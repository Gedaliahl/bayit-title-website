Updated 20 September 2026. This replaces an earlier version of this file that
put eleven questions to the First American agency representative. Most of those

## What happened

The eleven were sent out and answered from public research rather than by the
agency representative — the replies described what First American's public
materials do and do not establish, which is not the same as First American's
position on our files. Nine of the eleven resolved to "it depends on the facts,"
which is correct: underwriting is a file-by-file judgment and most of those
questions were not answerable in the general form they were asked.

Reading the replies against the drafts turned up the more useful point. **Seven
pages carried an `(underwriter/form)` flag, but five of them never asserted an
underwriting position in the first place.** They defer to it, in terms:

- `judgment` — "an affidavit … in a form the underwriter will accept," and "the
  form and the supporting documentation are First American's call, and we obtain
  their written sign-off on the affidavit before closing"
- `no-legal-access` — "An underwriter may offer an access endorsement in some
  circumstances, and may decline in others. It is an underwriting decision on
  the specific facts, not something to assume."
- `open-permits` — "the reason is structural rather than a matter of one
  underwriter's appetite," resting on the policy's building-and-zoning exclusion
  and Fla. Stat. § 162.09
- `bankruptcy` — "Whether the underwriter takes exception to anything specific
  depends on the order and the file."
- `litigation` — "Whether First American will insure over a recorded lis pendens
  at all, and on what terms, is theirs to decide file by file. We do not
  represent in advance that they will."

A page that defers does not need a bulletin to publish. It needs a statement of
our own practice, which is Category C. Those nine flags were rewritten
accordingly on 20 September 2026.

The two pages that genuinely did assert a First American position were the two I
had not flagged: `mobile-and-remote-signings` and
`foreign-seller-signing-from-abroad-florida` both reproduced First American's
published RON language and a fifteen-name list of approved platforms, drafted
from their public page rather than from a supplied bulletin. The foreign-seller
page went further and inferred from First American's silence that the position
covers a signer outside the United States. Both passages were cut. The pages'
RON claims now rest on Fla. Stat. §§ 117.209, 117.201(6) and 117.225, and on the
statement that we confirm lender and underwriter acceptance in writing before a
signing is scheduled — which is stronger, and which we can stand behind.

### One real bug — found 20 September, fixed the same day

**The calculator under-quoted every reissue.** R. 69O-186.003(2)(c) provides that
"any amount of new insurance, in the aggregate, in excess of the amount under the
previous policy shall be computed at the original owner's or leasehold rates".
`reissuePremium` applied the reissue brackets to the whole liability instead, and
nothing on the site asked what the previous policy was insured for. On a $500,000
sale with a $300,000 prior policy that was $1,530 against $1,930 — **$400 low**,
on a figure published as something a Loan Estimate can be built from, and low in
the common direction, since most properties are worth more now than when they
were last insured.

It is now modelled. `reissuePremium(liability, priorPolicyAmount)` rates the
layer up to the old policy at the reissue schedule and the excess at the
original one, both estimate modules pass the amount through, and the calculator
and the address estimator each show a "What the previous policy insured" field
when the reissue box is ticked. Leaving it empty still rates the whole liability
as reissue, and the line says so rather than implying a complete figure.
`tests/reissue-excess.test.ts` pins the arithmetic, the $100 minimum across the
split, and both notes.

**The interpretive choice inside it has been answered.** The rule says the excess
is rated at original rates "in the aggregate", and there were two ways to read
that: the excess sitting where it falls in the schedule once the whole liability
is considered, or the excess starting again at the first bracket as though it
were a fresh policy. On the example above that is $1,930 against $2,005.

Confirmed by the team on 20 September 2026: **the excess is rated at its position
in the aggregate schedule** — the tiers it occupies given the total new
liability — not as a standalone layer from the first bracket. That is what
`reissuePremium` already did, so nothing changed in the code; the answer is
recorded here and in the function's own comment so the next person does not have
to re-derive it. The contrast with (5)(a), which omits the aggregate wording and
is computed the other way in `simultaneousLoanPremium`, holds.

Noted for provenance: this is the team's confirmation, not a written underwriter
bulletin. It is the reading the rule's wording supports and the one the site now
prices on.

## If you do want to put something to the representative

Ask about a live file, not about policy in general. A rep will give a position on
"this seller, in this country, on this platform, closing on this date" and will
rarely give a general rule — which is why the pages are written to promise that
we get that answer in writing on each file, rather than to state the rule.
