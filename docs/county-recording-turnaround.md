# County recording turnaround — what each Florida recorder publishes

A sourced list of what every Florida county's recording office says, **in its own
words**, about how long recording takes. Built to clear the third open item on
the county page template (`app/counties/[slug]/page.tsx`):

> `'Typical recording turnaround at this clerk'`

**Checked: 14 September 2026.** Every quote below was read off the office's own
website on that date. Nothing here was taken from a search-engine summary, a
title-industry blog, or a recorder-directory site such as deeds.com — see
[Why the source matters](#why-the-source-matters), which documents a case where
those secondary sources were wrong.

---

## The rule this document follows

A county either publishes a turnaround statement or it does not. Where it does,
the statement is quoted verbatim with its URL. Where it does not, the entry says
so and lists the pages that were checked. **No county's timing was inferred from
a neighbouring county, from a vendor's marketing page, or from how long recording
"usually" takes.** 42 of the 67 counties publish nothing, and that is recorded as
a finding, not filled in.

Note the further limit: these are the offices' **published statements**, not
measured performance. "Typically the same day" is what the recorder advertises.
It is not a service level, and none of these offices guarantees it — several say
so explicitly. Written for a county page, the statement should be attributed to
the recorder, not asserted by us.

---

## The statewide point that is true in all 67 counties

Recording turnaround is an operational question, not a priority question. Under
[Fla. Stat. § 695.11](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0695/Sections/0695.11.html),
"Instruments deemed to be recorded from time of filing":

> "All instruments which are authorized or required to be recorded in the office
> of the clerk of the circuit court of any county in the State of Florida, and
> which are to be recorded in the 'Official Records' as provided for under
> s. 28.222 … shall be deemed to have been officially accepted by the said
> officer, and officially recorded, at the time she or he affixed thereon the
> consecutive official register numbers required under s. 28.222, and at such
> time shall be notice to all persons. The sequence of such official numbers
> shall determine the priority of recordation. An instrument bearing the lower
> number in the then-current series of numbers shall have priority over any
> instrument bearing a higher number in the same series."

Quoted from Online Sunshine, 14 September 2026. Priority runs from the official
register number, so what a delay affects is when the document appears in the
index and comes back to the file — not its place in the chain of title against
an instrument numbered later.

## Summary

| Counties | What they publish |
|---|---|
| **25** | A turnaround statement of some kind (quoted below) |
| **42** | Nothing about timing on the pages checked |

Only seven of the 25 put a number on how long recording itself takes for an
ordinary deed or mortgage: Lake, Lee and Pinellas (two business days),
Miami-Dade (one to three business days for e-recording), Seminole (24 to 72
hours), Orange (24 hours for e-recordings, three business days by mail — but see
the inconsistency noted in its entry), and Manatee (recorded the day received).
Flagler says only "within hours instead of days".

Five more publish a number that is **not** a recording turnaround and should not
be read as one: Okeechobee and St. Lucie quote seven to ten days for the paper
document to come back in the mail, and Sarasota, Hillsborough and Palm Beach give
figures for Notices of Commencement only.

The remainder state only that same-day recording is not guaranteed, or that
walking the document in is the way to get it.

## The three priority counties

Broward is in this section because it is a priority county, not because it
publishes a turnaround — it does not, and it is counted among the 42 below.

### Miami-Dade County — Clerk of the Court and Comptroller

> "We cannot guarantee documents will be recorded on the same day that we receive
> them."

> "Documents submitted via eRecording may take approximately 1–3 business days

> available for public viewing."

Source: [miamidadeclerk.gov — Official Records](https://www.miamidadeclerk.gov/clerk/official-records.page)

### Palm Beach County — Clerk of the Circuit Court & Comptroller

> "We cannot guarantee documents will be recorded on the same day that we receive
> them."

> "Notices of Commencement are normally recorded within one business day when
> submitted electronically."

Walk-in recording is offered under a heading "Same Day Recording":

> "Same Day Recording"
>
> "Except for designated holidays. Present documents in person at the following
> locations, Monday - Friday, 8 a.m. - 4 p.m."

(five locations listed: Main Courthouse, South County, North County, West County,
Royal Palm Beach)

Source: [mypalmbeachclerk.com — Recording](https://www.mypalmbeachclerk.com/departments/recording)
and [eRecording](https://www.mypalmbeachclerk.com/departments/recording/erecording).
The live site refuses automated requests; both pages were read from the Internet
Archive's 2025 capture of the clerk's own pages. **Worth re-reading by hand
before it goes on a page**, since the capture may trail the live text.

### Broward County — Records, Taxes and Treasury Division

**No turnaround statement published.** Separately worth correcting in the
`locations` table: recording in Broward is not done by the Clerk of Courts. The
county's own description:

> "RTT is the statutory repository for the Official Records of the County."

> "RTT is responsible for collecting tourist development taxes and recording
> documents into the County's official Land Records and do tax deed auctions."

Source: [broward.org — Records, Taxes and Treasury](https://www.broward.org/recordstaxestreasury).
The Recording Office is at 115 S. Andrews Ave., Room 114, Fort Lauderdale.
Pages checked for timing, all silent on it: the division landing page,
[Recording a Document](https://www.broward.org/RecordsTaxesTreasury/Records/Pages/RecordingaDocument.aspx),
[Electronic Recording](https://www.broward.org/RecordsTaxesTreasury/Records/Pages/ElectronicRecording.aspx),
and [the Recording section page](https://www.broward.org/RecordsTaxesTreasury/RecordingClerk/Pages/default.aspx).
`locations.clerk_name` currently reads "Broward County Clerk of the Circuit and
County Courts", which is the wrong office for a recording question.

## Counties that publish nothing about timing

42 counties. Their recording and e-recording pages were read on 14 September 2026
and say nothing about how long recording takes. For these the county page should
keep the open item rather than carry a number.

Alachua, Baker, Bay, Bradford, Brevard, **Broward**, Calhoun, Clay, Columbia,
DeSoto, Dixie, Escambia, Franklin, Gadsden, Gilchrist, Glades, Gulf, Hamilton,
Hardee, Hendry, Hernando, Holmes, Indian River, Jackson, Jefferson, Lafayette,
Levy, Liberty, Madison, Marion, Martin, Monroe, Nassau, Pasco, Polk, St. Johns,
Santa Rosa, Sumter, Suwannee, Union, Wakulla, Washington.

Some of these publish a claim about e-recording being *faster* without ever
saying how long it takes. Escambia is typical — its list of "Benefits of
eRecording" includes "Expedites recording process" and "Reduces time delay for
recording"
([escambiaclerk.com](https://escambiaclerk.com/257/E-Recording-Help-and-Information)).
That is marketing copy about a direction of travel, not a turnaround, and it
cannot be turned into one.

A handful of the sites in this list — Baker, Dixie, Gadsden, Union, Wakulla,
Lafayette, Madison — are small and thin enough that a phone call to the recording
office would settle the question faster than any amount of further reading.

## Why the source matters

While checking Lake County, a search-result summary drawn from a third-party
recorder directory reported the turnaround as "4–5 business days." The Lake
County Clerk's own recording page says:

> "Documents are recorded in the order received, typically within two (2)
> business days."

Had the secondary source been used, this site would have published a figure that
is more than double what the recorder states, under a licensed agency's byline.
Every entry above was therefore read off the office's own page. Where the live
site refuses automated requests (Palm Beach, Lee, Pinellas, Sarasota), the
Internet Archive's capture of that same official page was used and is flagged in
the entry.

## Two other cautions

**Shared boilerplate.** Several of these sentences are identical across counties
because the offices adopted the same vendor-supplied e-recording copy. "Documents
are typically recorded the same day if submitted during regular business hours"
appears verbatim in Charlotte, Citrus, Highlands and Orange; "There is no
guarantee that documents will be recorded by Clerk of Court in the same day as it
is e-Recorded" in Okaloosa, Taylor and Putnam; "We cannot guarantee documents
will be recorded on the same day that we receive them" in Miami-Dade, Palm Beach,
Volusia, Lee and Walton; and the Okeechobee and St. Lucie FAQ answers are word
for word the same. Each is still that office's own published statement, but the
wording is not evidence that the office measured anything.

**What the statement is about.** Several counties' only published number is for
Notices of Commencement (Sarasota, Hillsborough, and Palm Beach's one-business-day
line), and Okeechobee's and St. Lucie's seven-to-ten days is the return of the
paper document, not the recording. Read each entry before reusing its number for
a deed or a mortgage.

## Using this on a county page

The county template lists three open items, of which this document clears the
third for 25 counties. Suggested shape, using the recorder's words:

> The {office} states that {verbatim statement}. Recording turnaround affects
> when a policy can issue, so it is worth knowing on a file with a tight
> timeline. Under Fla. Stat. § 695.11 an instrument is recorded, and is notice to
> all persons, when the clerk affixes the official register number — priority
> runs from that number, not from when the image appears in the index.

For the 42 counties with nothing published, leave
`'Typical recording turnaround at this clerk'` in the open-items list.

Nothing in this document should reach a published page until a licensed reviewer
has confirmed it, per the review rule in `docs/claude-project-brief.md`. These
pages also change without notice; a statement worth publishing is worth
re-checking against its URL at the page's next review date.
