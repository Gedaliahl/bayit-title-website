## In our office

The closing happens at 3301 N University Drive in Coral Springs. Documents are on the table, the closer walks through them, and questions get answered by the person who prepared the file rather than relayed.

Office hours are Monday through Thursday, 9:00 to 5:00, and Friday 9:00 to 12:00. Signings outside those hours are arranged in advance on a specific file; we do not staff the office after hours as a standing matter.

## Wherever the signer is

We send a notary to the signer — a kitchen table, an office, a hospital, a closing in another county. This is the ordinary answer when a signer cannot travel or when the schedule does not permit a trip to Coral Springs.

## Online, by remote online notarization

Florida authorises remote online notarization: the signer appears before a Florida-commissioned online notary by audio-video link, with identity verification and a recorded session. For a signer out of state or out of the country, this is often the practical route.

The act has to be performed by a notary who holds a current Florida notary commission **and** is separately registered with the Department of State as an online notary public. [Fla. Stat. § 117.225](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0117/Sections/0117.225.html) sets out what that registration requires: the underlying commission, a course covering the duties and the technology, a $25,000 bond, and errors and omissions coverage of at least $25,000. A standard Florida notary commission on its own does not authorise a remote online notarization.

Two things decide whether RON can be used on a given file, and neither is up to the signer: the lender has to accept remotely notarized documents, and so does the underwriter. We confirm both before scheduling rather than after.

First American approves RON "subject to state laws and all other First American underwriting guidelines and requirements," and publishes the platforms it accepts — Blend, Docutech, DocVerify, Nexsys, NotaryCam, NotaryLive, Pavaso, ProNotary, Proof, Qualia, Signix, Simplifile, SimplySecureSign, Snapdocs and Stavvy — together with any vendor on the MISMO RON Certified Providers list and platforms that integrate with those vendors.

The exclusions that bite in practice come from the statute rather than the underwriter. Solemnising marriage is outside RON under [Fla. Stat. § 117.285](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0117/Sections/0117.285.html), and electronic witnessing is not valid where the principal is a vulnerable adult as defined in s. 415.102.

## Which one should we use?

Whichever removes the constraint on your file. A seller in another country is a RON or consulate question. A buyer who cannot leave work is a mobile signing. A first-time buyer who wants to sit down and go through the documents is usually best served in the office. There is no premium version — these are three ways of doing the same closing.

## When should an attorney be involved?

Where someone is signing under a power of attorney, where a signer's capacity is in question, or where a party wants advice on the documents themselves rather than an explanation of what they are. We can tell you what a document does in the transaction; we are not lawyers and we do not advise on whether to sign it.

## Common questions

### Is a remote online notarization as valid as an in-person one?

It is a notarial act authorised by Florida law with its own identity verification and recording requirements.

It is authorised by part II of chapter 117, [Fla. Stat. §§ 117.201](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0117/Sections/0117.201.html)–117.305. [Fla. Stat. § 117.209](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0117/Sections/0117.209.html)(1) lets an online notary perform any function authorised under part I of the chapter as an online notarization, other than solemnising the rites of matrimony. Subsection (2) provides that where a notarial act requires the principal to appear before the notary, the principal may appear by audio-video communication technology meeting the statute's requirements.

The limits worth knowing: the notary must be **physically in Florida**, though the signer need not be (§ 117.209(3)); the notary must register separately with the Department of State as an online notary under [Fla. Stat. § 117.225](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0117/Sections/0117.225.html), on top of holding a standard commission; identity is established by personal knowledge, or by credential analysis of a government-issued ID plus knowledge-based authentication, under [Fla. Stat. § 117.265](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0117/Sections/0117.265.html); and a principal may not act as a witness to their own signature.

### Can everyone on the file sign a different way?

Usually yes. A buyer signing in the office and a seller signing remotely is common.

### What does the signer need for a RON session?

A device with a camera and microphone, a stable connection, and valid identification for the verification step.

The statute sets the floor, and every approved platform implements it. Under [Fla. Stat. § 117.265](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0117/Sections/0117.265.html), unless the notary has personal knowledge of the signer, identity is established by all three of: the signer presenting a government-issued identification credential remotely over the audio-video link; **credential analysis** of that credential; and **identity proofing** in the form of knowledge-based authentication — a short set of questions generated from public and proprietary data, answered within a time limit.

For a signer outside the United States, a passport issued by a foreign government without a US immigration stamp may be used as the credential.

What varies by platform is the mechanics — how many attempts the knowledge-based authentication allows, and how a failed attempt is retried. We tell the signer what to have ready before the session rather than at the start of it.
